# Reporting standards

This portfolio separates **engineering evidence** from **leadership reporting**.

## Engineering report

Create one technical report after a meaningful build, configuration change, or investigation. It should answer:

- **Objective:** What security or IAM problem did this solve?
- **Design:** What systems and trust boundaries were involved?
- **Implementation:** What was configured and why?
- **Validation:** Which test, log, query or screenshot proves the result?
- **Risks/limitations:** What remains untested or out of scope?
- **Next action:** What must be validated, remediated or built next?

## CISO-level update

Use a concise, decision-oriented summary for major milestones, noteworthy risks, incidents or remediation outcomes. State **risk, business impact, current control posture, evidence quality, owner/action and decision needed**. Avoid a click-by-click portal diary. A CISO update may be unnecessary for a minor lab exercise.

## Evidence maturity labels

- **Planned:** not yet attempted.
- **Reported complete:** performed according to the lab owner, without attached test evidence.
- **Validated:** objective results or sanitized artifacts attached with dates.
- **Limitations recorded:** known telemetry gaps, insufficient rights, test constraints or unverified assumptions are listed.

Do not promote a reported result to validated without recording the evidence. This is an educational training environment, not a production security attestation.
